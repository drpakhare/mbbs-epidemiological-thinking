What the lecture covers

  1. Two coronaviruses, two outcomes: SARS in 2003 and COVID-19 in 2020
  2. Natural history and its stages: susceptibility, subclinical disease, clinical disease, outcome, and the clinical horizon
  3. How we learned natural history, and what it cost: Oslo, Tuskegee and untreated HIV
  4. The iceberg of infection: infectivity, pathogenicity and virulence
  5. The hidden reservoir: inapparent infection, carriers and latent infection
  6. Incubation, latent and infectious periods, serial interval, and why presymptomatic spread defeats isolation
  7. Isolation and quarantine, from Ragusa in 1377 to 2020
  8. Cervical cancer: a chronic disease with a long silent stage, the screening window and lead time
  9. Levels of prevention: definitions, Leavell and Clark's five modes of intervention, nine diseases from heart disease to suicide, and common viva traps
